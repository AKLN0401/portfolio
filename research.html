Research · MEMS · CMOS · Semiconductor Fabrication

Microfabrication & sensor research

Hands-on semiconductor and MEMS fabrication in a Class 10 cleanroom, working across photolithography, ion implantation, dry etching, deposition, wafer processing, and device characterization.

At the Nano-Systems Fabrication Laboratory, I work directly with the process steps that turn a bare wafer into a functioning device. That means running lithography and etch steps myself, tracking process parameters across a full fabrication sequence, and characterizing the resulting devices rather than only reading about the process.

A parallel thread of this work is a research paper, done with a professor, on a variable D-Q delay flip-flop for delay-fault detection on an RV32IM RISC-V core, implemented in GlobalFoundries' 12LP+ (GF12LPplus) 12 nm process. The flip-flop's data-to-output delay is made deliberately adjustable, which lets it be used to catch delay faults that standard scan-based DFT can miss.

Together, the fabrication and circuit-level work reflect the same underlying interest: understanding hardware failure and reliability from the physical device up through the digital logic built on top of it.

Process steps

01 / CLEANROOM

  1. Photolithography

    Patterning photoresist to define device geometry on the wafer surface.

  2. Ion implantation

    Introducing dopants to set device electrical properties at specific depths and concentrations.

  3. Dry etching (RIE / ICP)

    Removing material selectively to transfer patterned geometry into functional structures.

  4. Deposition (PVD / CVD)

    Building up thin films — conductors, dielectrics, and structural layers — across the wafer.

  5. Wafer processing & characterization

    Tracking the full process sequence and verifying device outcomes with SEM, profilometry, and ellipsometry.
