NVIDIA NVDLA 12 nm: 1.1 GHz, Multi-Vt, Full Scan

NVIDIA’s open-source deep-learning accelerator, taken from RTL to a foundry-clean layout under a licensed 12 nm FinFET PDK, with power, test and floorplan symmetry built in from the start.

Target 0.909 ns (1.1 GHz) at the slow corner with 10% supply droop, checked hot and cold, on a 13-metal stack with three threshold-voltage flavours. Signoff is the foundry’s own DRC and LVS on the merged GDS. The SRAM macros are generated, and synthesis is under way, starting with partition A.

How NVDLA works

ARCHITECTURE

NVDLA (NVIDIA Deep Learning Accelerator) is a fixed-function inference engine that NVIDIA open-sourced in 2017. A host CPU programs it through a register interface (CSB), points it at feature maps and weights in memory, and the hardware runs a layer (convolution, activation, pooling, normalization) without the CPU touching the data.

The convolution core does most of the work. CDMA streams feature-map data and weights from memory into the on-chip convolution buffer (CBUF). The sequencer (CSC) reads them back in the order the math needs and feeds the MAC array (CMAC), split into two identical halves. CACC sums partial results across channels.

Results then flow through the post-processing pipeline: SDP applies per-element operations (bias, batch norm, ReLU/PReLU, element-wise add), PDP does pooling, and CDP does cross-channel normalization. BDMA and RUBIK copy and reshape tensors between layers.

For physical design, NVDLA is cut into five partitions, each synthesized and laid out on its own. Partition C carries the convolution buffer: 128 dual-port SRAM macros.

Low power, test and PPA

IMPLEMENTATION

All five partitions share one synthesis script and one place-and-route script, so each gets the same treatment.

Three threshold voltages

in synthesis

The slow, regular and fast Vt flavours are loaded together. Optimization is weighted toward leakage, so cells stay on the slow flavour by default and switch to a faster one only where a path needs the speed. Hold fixing uses slow-flavour delay cells only.

Clock gating

in synthesis

NVDLA’s own RTL clock gates are imported as real gating cells, and synthesis adds more where 3 to 64 flops share an enable. Scan-enable is defined before the gates go in, so every gate’s test pin is driven by it rather than tied to a constant.

Scan chains

in synthesis

Muxed scan inserted by synthesis from the library’s own scan flops: 128 chains per partition behind on-chip compression, so each block needs only 8 scan-in and 8 scan-out pins and the assembled design still fits a pad ring. Written out as SCANDEF so placement can reorder each chain by location. Each netlist is checked by Conformal equivalence and by Modus chain tracing and ATPG.

PPA optimization

applied

Genus 25.1 at its highest optimization effort, with physical libraries and the extraction tech file loaded so cell sizes and wire estimates are real. Timing carries margin from the first run: clock uncertainty for jitter and skew, a flat on-chip-variation derate, and a boundary budget on every partition port, checked at both slow corners. The clock period stays fixed: misses get fixed in the design or the flow, not by relaxing the target.

SRAM for 13 metals

generated

Both CBUF macro sizes were generated with the memory compiler, with timing views at every signoff corner. A macro uses only the lower metal layers and blocks them; everything above routes straight over it, so the same layout works on the 13-metal stack.

Signoff corners

set

Setup at the slow corner with 10% supply droop, hot and cold, since at FinFET voltages cold can be the slower case. Hold at the fast corner with 10% over-voltage, also hot and cold. Power at the typical corner, 85 °C.

Place-and-route plan

FLOORPLAN

Each partition is placed and routed on its own as a hard macro, then the block is assembled from those macros. The floorplan is mirror-symmetric: one MAC partition layout is used twice, with MB flipped.

The arrangement comes from counting every signal between partitions in NVDLA’s top-level netlist. The two MAC partitions are the same module, and their connections mirror each other port for port: every MA port that goes to O goes to C on MB (2,423 bits), and every MA port to P goes to A on MB (1,434 bits). So MA and MB become full-height strips on the outer edges, one layout used twice, and the rest stacks in the centre: C on top, O in the middle, P and A side by side at the bottom. That gives the big links shared edges: O–P (6,280 bits), C–O (5,117) and P–A (1,950).

C straddles the mirror axis, so its 128 SRAM macros are symmetric too: two banks of eight identical columns on its left and right, one the mirror image of the other, with the CDMA and CSC logic between them. Columns pair up so their pin edges face a shared channel. A macro blocks only the lower metal layers, so C’s edge pins, including the 2,423 bits to MB, route over the columns into the logic, and the bottom edge stays free for the 5,117 bits to O. O’s right edge is free as well, so the 2,662 bits of chip-level ports split evenly between both side channels.

Planned floorplan, to scale from block-area estimates. Ribbons: shared edges and the signals crossing them. Dotted: A–C, 106 bits, the only link without a shared edge, routed through the side channel.

Power grid

Each block carries its own grid from the cell rails up to the top two power layers, which also become the block’s power pins. The top-level mesh lands directly on them. Every layer pair in the grid crosses at right angles, so every crossing gets a via.

Routing

Signals route on the layers below the power mesh, with timing- and crosstalk-driven routing, antenna diodes, and up to three automatic repair passes on any design-rule marker. The clock uses double-width, double-space wires on the middle layers.

Foundry signoff

The router’s own checker is not the signoff. The merged GDS goes through the foundry DRC deck after double-patterning colouring and metal fill, then LVS against the routed netlist.

Next

ROADMAP

  1. Synthesize all five partitions

    M first as a check of the flow, then A, C, O and P, each at 0.909 ns. Check each netlist with Conformal equivalence against the RTL and Modus chain tracing and ATPG.

  2. Block place-and-route

    C first, as it holds all 128 SRAM macros: init, placement with scan reorder, clock tree, routing and signoff timing on every corner. Then each block is exported as a hard macro: layout, abstract and timing models.

  3. Block assembly

    Six macros in the symmetric floorplan, the MAC partition used twice, the top-level clock balanced against each block’s internal clock latency, and the inter-partition buses routed through the channels.

  4. Foundry signoff

    Double-patterning colouring, metal fill, foundry DRC to zero, then LVS on the merged GDS.

Under the licensed 12 nm PDK, the foundry, library and cell names, memory-compiler settings and absolute area and power are not published here.
