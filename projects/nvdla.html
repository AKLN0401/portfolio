NVIDIA NVDLA 12 nm: 1.1 GHz, Multi-Vt, Full Scan

Implementing NVIDIA’s open-source deep-learning accelerator from RTL to a foundry-clean layout in a 12 nm FinFET flow, at 1.1 GHz with power, test and symmetry built in from the start.

NVDLA (NVIDIA Deep Learning Accelerator) is an open-source inference engine. The full configuration used here has two multiply–accumulate arrays, a convolution buffer built from 128 dual-port SRAM macros, and post-processing engines for activation, pooling and normalization. It is a self-directed project, using tool and PDK access provided through the CMOS Design and Reliability Group.

The goal is a block that would pass a real tape-out review: timing met at the slow corner on every view, hold met at the fast corner, scan in place, and the merged GDS clean under the foundry’s own DRC and LVS decks.

Flow

01 / PLAN
  • Synthesis at highest effort, one run per partition, with physical libraries loaded so the netlist sees real cell sizes.
  • Multi-Vt: three threshold-voltage flavours. The leakage-weighted default uses the slowest flavour and spends the fast one only where timing needs it.
  • Clock gating inserted by synthesis, with every gate’s test pin tied to scan-enable.
  • Full scan: muxed-scan chains inserted by synthesis and reordered during placement, checked by ATPG.
  • SRAM macros generated for the 13-metal stack. Only the lower layers are used inside the macro, so the upper layers route over it.
  • Hierarchical hard macros: each partition is placed and routed on its own, and the two CMAC copies share one layout.
  • Hold fixed with low-leakage delay cells at the fast corner, at both temperatures.
  • Foundry signoff: DRC with double-patterning colouring and metal fill, then LVS, on the merged GDS.

Floorplan

02 / SYMMETRY

The top level instantiates six partitions, and the two CMAC halves are the same module. That makes a mirror-symmetric floorplan natural: one CMAC layout is placed twice, the second copy flipped, on either side of the convolution core.

Partition c sits on the centre line with its SRAM banks in two mirrored columns. Their pins face inward, so the read paths to both CMAC halves have the same length. The memory interface runs across the top, and accumulation and post-processing sit side by side along the bottom.

Every block keeps its own power grid and exposes it on the top two power layers. The top-level mesh lands directly on those pins, and the channels between blocks carry the inter-partition buses.

Disclosure

03 / SCOPE

The RTL is NVIDIA’s open-source NVDLA. The work is done under licensed academic access to a 12 nm FinFET PDK. The foundry, PDK revision, library and cell names, memory-compiler settings and any absolute area or power figures are not published here. Results will be added as normalized figures as each stage closes.
