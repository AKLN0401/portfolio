RISC-V Fault Detection

Evaluated hardware fault-detection approaches on an RV32IM processor, including DFT instrumentation, ATPG coverage, timing closure, area overhead, and power impact.

This project focused on an RV32IM RISC-V processor as the vehicle for evaluating fault-detection instrumentation, rather than treating the processor as a fixed black box. Adding DFT structures to a working processor core means every change has to be weighed against its cost to the design.

The evaluation looked at ATPG (automatic test pattern generation) coverage achieved by the inserted structures, how much timing closure was affected once the DFT logic was in place, and the resulting area and power overhead relative to the un-instrumented core.

The goal throughout was to understand the trade-off space directly: how much fault coverage a given DFT approach buys, and what it costs in area, power, and timing margin on a real RISC-V core rather than an idealized model.

CoreRV32IM RISC-V processor
InstrumentationDFT structures for fault detection
Evaluated metricsATPG coverage, timing closure, area overhead, power impact

Evaluation criteria

01 / TRADE-OFFS

  1. DFT instrumentation

    Inserting fault-detection structures into the RV32IM core.

  2. ATPG coverage

    Measuring the fraction of modeled faults the generated test patterns detect.

  3. Timing closure

    Checking whether the instrumented core still meets timing under the target constraints.

  4. Area overhead

    Comparing instrumented core area against the baseline core.

  5. Power impact

    Assessing the additional power drawn by the fault-detection logic.
