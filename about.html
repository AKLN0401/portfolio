About

What happens between a device on a wafer and a working system.

I am interested in what happens between a device on a wafer and a working hardware system. That has led me toward digital IC design first, and toward semiconductor fabrication alongside it — I like being able to trace a problem from a transistor's physical behavior all the way up to how a chip performs under load.

I'm a fourth-year electrical engineering student at the University of Manitoba. From April to September 2026 I was an ASIC design co-op and visiting research fellow in Prof. Manoj Sachdev's CMOS Design and Reliability Group at the University of Waterloo, working on backend physical design, DFT and tapeout for a multi-block RISC-V SoC in a 12-nm FinFET process. Before that, from May 2025 to April 2026, I was a student researcher at the Nano-Systems Fabrication Laboratory at Manitoba, running cleanroom processing and device characterization for CMOS transistors and for flexible and MEMS sensors.

I care about the reliability side of hardware as much as the design side. A circuit that works in simulation and a circuit that survives years of operation are different problems, and a lot of my project work — DFT architectures, delay-fault detection, timing closure under realistic conditions — sits in that gap.

Outside of coursework and lab hours, I spend time reading process and device physics papers, rebuilding flows I don't fully understand yet until I do, and looking for the next project that forces me to learn a new tool in the ASIC or fabrication stack.
